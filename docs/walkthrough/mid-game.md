# Mid game: Cambria, Alma and Grumm

!!! warning "Full spoilers ahead"
    This page spoils quests, characters and plot triggers. If you want a gentler
    start, read [How to use the walkthrough](index.md) first. The previous stage is
    covered in [Early game: Drassen, Chitzena and San Mona](early-game.md).

By now you should hold Drassen (and have survived the counterattack), probably
Chitzena, and have picked San Mona clean. The mid game is about the three big
towns in the center and west of Arulco: **Cambria** (money, healing and
shopping), **Alma** (the Queen's military heartland and the doorway to the
endgame plot) and **Grumm** (the industrial powerhouse with the toughest
defenses so far).

Everything below describes the standard campaign, which 1.13 keeps intact.
Where 1.13 changes something, it is flagged explicitly.

## Planning the mid game

A sensible order is **Cambria first, then Alma, then Grumm**:

- Cambria sits in the center of the country, with roads leading to every other
  city except Balime. It gives you another mine, the only hospital in Arulco and
  a general store — but that same central position makes it a prime target for
  enemy counterattacks, so plan to defend it.
- Alma is the army's headquarters and training center. Expect concentrated
  resistance in every sector, plus patrols roaming the surrounding jungle and
  farmland that quickly reinforce or retake captured sectors. Alma also holds
  the clue that reveals Orta, which you will want for the
  [late game](late-game.md).
- Grumm is a coastal factory town. Entry by land is limited — every road into
  Grumm passes through its mine sector (H3) first, and the only other way in is
  the warehouse district (G2), because half the city is surrounded by water.

!!! note "The difficulty ramp is real in 1.13"
    In 1.13, the gear enemy soldiers carry improves with your **campaign
    progress** (not elapsed time) — the "Progress Speed of Item Choices" setting
    on the [New Game screen](../playing/new-game-options.md) controls how fast,
    and it affects your militia's equipment too. On Expert, garrisons are 20%
    larger with 25% more elites than the default; on Insane, the Queen's
    reinforcements are unlimited and her attacks never taper off (by default —
    both can be changed in `JA2_Options.ini`, see
    [the options tour](../configuration/options-ini.md)). Mid game is where this
    ramp becomes noticeable: loot the better weapons and armor that garrison and
    SAM-site troops start dropping, and keep upgrading.

## Cambria

Five sectors: **F8, F9, G8, G9, H8**. Cambria is where the doctors live, and
its silver mine in H8 adds a solid income stream.

| Sector | What's there |
|--------|--------------|
| F8 | Cambria Hospital — Dr. Vince Beaumont, Dr. Steve Willis, Nurse Jenny |
| F9 | Cambria University (shut down by the Queen, now ruins) |
| G8 | Residential district — Martha Graham's house |
| G9 | Commercial district — Keith's store, Perko's repair shop, Rat |
| H8 | Cambria silver mine and Mining Commission building |

### Taking the town

- **F8 (hospital)**: a large building with many corridors and rooms. Most
  defenders are inside; a few patrol outside near the garbage bins, and one
  hides in the warehouse on the west end — careful, it is full of explosives.
  There are two entrances into the central hallway: the south "front" entrance
  gets you to the heart of the building fastest, the north "rear" entrance has
  more doors to clear. The many windows let defenders take potshots at you, and
  there are lots of civilians about, so watch your fire and go easy on
  explosives.
- **F9 (university)**: the campus is ringed by solid concrete walls you can
  climb over. The sector has almost no lighting, so night assaults work very
  well here. Land mines litter the grounds — most are duds you can pick up, but
  one live mine is buried in the mouth of a one-tile hole in the wall on the
  south side of the cafeteria. Bring someone with high Wisdom or Explosives, or
  a metal detector.
- **G8 (residential)**: a mix of buildings and wilderness. Most enemies cluster
  around the built-up area, with a few in the woods and behind the rocks near
  the northeast roads. Approach from the west or south to use the buildings as
  cover.
- **G9 (commercial)**: a tight urban fight. Enemies bunker down in the stores
  and houses, with a large concentration around the ACA building in the center.
- **H8 (mine)**: heavily defended — the Mining Commission building and a small
  shack on the north end draw most of the garrison. The sector is dark at
  night; one classic tactic is to come in from the east side of the northern
  edge, climb onto the Mining Commission roof and fire down on the defenders.
  Watch for enemies on the storehouse roof.

Once the whole town is yours, expect the Queen to fight long and hard to take
it back before she turns her attention elsewhere. Train militia early (see
[Militia](../playing/features/militia.md)).

### The hospital

The hospital in F8 is unique: its doctors will treat your wounded mercs for
cash. The rules:

- You must **fully liberate Cambria** before the doctors will treat anyone.
- The first time — or while town loyalty is low — they charge full price. The
  cost drops as you do good deeds for the town.
- After you complete the Find Joey quest (below), a doctor will treat you for
  free once, and at a heavy discount afterwards.
- At 100% town loyalty, treatment becomes free — but only if you behaved.

!!! danger "Do not steal the medical supplies"
    Taking the hospital's medical supplies raises treatment prices, locks you
    out of the free-treatment bonus, and makes it **impossible to recruit
    Dr. Vince Beaumont**. Leave the crates alone.

### Martha and Joey (Find Joey)

Martha Graham, in her house in the western part of G8, asks you to find her
fifteen-year-old son Joey, who ran away three days ago. She won't ask the army
for help — they would just draft him.

Joey is in **San Mona**: either peeking in the windows behind the Shady Lady
brothel (C5) or nosing around the abandoned mine west of town (D4). Hans (or
the bartender) in San Mona has usually seen him and will point you the right
way (see [San Mona in the early-game chapter](early-game.md)).

Joey is a brat and refuses to come along unless you **threaten** him. Once he
agrees, escort him straight back to Martha in G8 — if a fight breaks out on the
way, park him somewhere safe (a windowless room, behind a rock) until the
sector is clear.

Rewards: a 20% loyalty jump in Cambria, working hospital treatment, and one of
the preconditions for recruiting Vince.

### Recruiting Dr. Vince Beaumont

Vince is a surgeon at the hospital who despises the Queen — the staff say he
talks about taking down the regime himself. Once Cambria's loyalty is close to
100% (finish Find Joey and clear out the Hicks) and you kept your hands off the
medical supplies, a merc with high Leadership can convince him to join for
**$400/day**.

He arrives with the highest starting Medical stat of any recruitable character
in the game, backed by very high Wisdom, the Teaching skill and ambidexterity —
but poor Marksmanship and Agility, so keep him in the back. He is
claustrophobic (keep him above ground) and an optimist (his morale holds up).
In 1.13 he comes with the **Paramedic** trait, making him an even better
dedicated medic. See [Recruitable NPCs](npcs-recruitment.md) for his full
stats.

### Keith's store and the Hicks

Keith Hemps runs the general store in G9 (he shows up around 9:00 AM). He needs
roughly **35% town loyalty** before he will deal with you at all. He sells
cheap secondhand goods — a good source of glass jars, toolkits, first aid kits
and canteens — and buys most items at good prices. Two catches: he only holds
**$1,500 in cash**, refreshed every 24 hours, and he won't touch weapons.

Why no weapons? The **Hicks** — a lawless hillbilly clan on a farm in **F10**,
east of the university — steal every gun he stocks. Keith asks you to deal with
them ("Eliminate the Hicks"), and if you let the problem fester too long he
goes out of business entirely.

There is no peaceful solution: you have to kill the whole family. They carry
shotguns and Ruger Mini-14s that hit hard up close, but they wear no helmets
and little armor, have no night-vision gear, and never throw grenades — so
headshots and night attacks are very effective. A proven tactic is to fort up
in a small windowless room and let them funnel through the doorway into your
interrupts.

!!! warning "Ladies, don't marry Daryl"
    If a female merc talks to Daryl Hick, he proposes and invites her into "the
    family". Accept and you can take the Hicks' weapon stash without a fight —
    but you **lose that merc permanently** (she cannot be re-hired, even after
    you wipe out the Hicks) and the town's problem remains unsolved.

Rewards: a Cambria loyalty boost, the Hicks' weapon stash, and Keith starts
buying and selling guns and armor (he pays the best prices in the game for
Guardian Vests, within his cash limit).

### Perko's repair shop

Alex "Perko" Perkolopolis, also in G9, repairs damaged equipment for a fee. He
is sloppy and frequently misses his own deadlines; he works faster if you hand
items in shortly after midnight and collect them one at a time. He refuses to
touch electronics — for those he sends you to Fredo in Grumm (see below).

!!! tip
    Repairing badly worn gear often costs more than the item would sell for.
    Pay Perko only for items you actually intend to keep, and sell the rest —
    or better, bring your own mechanic.

### The mine

The H8 silver mine works like the others: the head miner runs it and assigns
the workers, and its daily income scales with Cambria's loyalty — so the Joey
and Hicks quests literally pay for themselves. If the mine ever falls back into
the Queen's hands, the miners go back to working for her. While you are down
there, look around: silver nuggets lying on the mine floor can be picked up and
deposited like cash.

## Alma

Four sectors: **H13, H14, I13, I14**. Alma is the military headquarters of
Arulco and the main training facility for the Queen's army — a hybrid of a town
and a base, teeming with soldiers, munitions and barricades. There is a silver
mine in I14 run by head miner Matt Duncan.

| Sector | What's there |
|--------|--------------|
| H13 | Military HQ and shooting range — Sergeant Krott, Lt. Conrad Gillitt, General Theo Humphey |
| H14 | Storage depot (two huge warehouses) |
| I13 | Barracks and prison (captured mercs end up here) |
| I14 | Alma silver mine and residential area — Auntie, Matt Duncan |

### Taking the town

- **H13 (military HQ)**: most soldiers are inside the building or out front
  near the southwest car entrance, so approach from the north or east; the tree
  cover behind the base makes the lone roof guard an easy first target. Two
  guards at the southwest entryway never move until they see you — expect them
  to be your last stragglers.
- **H14 (storage depot)**: nearly every enemy is inside the two massive
  warehouses, plus one guard on each flat roof. The big sliding doors are
  electronically locked **and trapped** — the only untrapped ways in are the
  small doors at the far north end of each building. A switch in the small
  office on the north side of the northern building opens every vertical
  sliding door in the sector: clear the roofs, line your squad up on top, flip
  the switch and shoot the gas tanks and rockets stored inside (anything caught
  in the blast is destroyed, so weigh loot against safety).
- **I13 (barracks and prison)**: the facility sits behind a wire fence with no
  convenient gaps — bring wire cutters or a grenade, or go over the roofs. If
  the alarm is raised, the guards flood the prison area with mustard gas; that
  only matters if one of your people is locked up inside, in which case reach
  the central security room before being spotted. Several lockers and chests
  here are trapped — shooting the locks is safer than picking them.
- **I14 (mine)**: come in from the north (from H14) and fight from behind the
  row of houses along the northern street; the flat-roofed liquor store on that
  street is a perfect sniper perch covering the whole road. Watch the
  fenced-off woods in the northwest — a group of three or four soldiers usually
  sits in there, unable to come out but with a clear line on that rooftop.

!!! note "1.13: the mine garrison sends help"
    In 1.13, if you attack the storage depot (H14) first, be ready for
    reinforcements streaming in from the mine sector — the mine always has
    extra soldiers due to its strategic importance, and they will come to a
    neighboring sector under attack.

### Sergeant Krott and the Rocket Rifles

The shooting range at the back of the H13 base is where Sergeant Krott works —
and where a **Rocket Rifle**, one of the Queen's experimental weapons, is kept.
As soon as the base goes on alert, someone presses the red button in the
command room, destroying the stash and wounding Krott. To claim a Rocket Rifle
intact, secure the command room **without being spotted**.

Krott himself has no stomach for the army — he just wants to go home to his
family. Let him leave (patch him up first if the blast injured him) and he
promises to spread word of your deeds, raising Alma's loyalty. That matters:
Alma's loyalty is hard-won, and the mine income depends on it.

### Recruiting Lt. Conrad Gillitt

Conrad is a Hungarian mercenary who trained recruits for the army at H13 under
General Humphey. His contract has expired, Deidranna kept paying him late, and
he is open to offers — from a merc with a modestly high Leadership. He asks
**$5,500/day**; if your high-Leadership merc refuses the first offer, he drops
to **$3,300/day**.

Two warnings: he is easily bored, and if you keep him talking too long he
decides killing you sounds more interesting — make your offer promptly. He is
also a non-swimmer, so keep him away from deep water.

He is worth the money. In 1.13 Conrad is a full **Machinegunner**
(auto-weapons expert) with 95 Marksmanship, and he has the Teaching skill —
which is worth even more in 1.13's trait system, where Teaching only has one
level — making him one of the best militia trainers and town defenders you can
get. Full stats on the [Recruitable NPCs page](npcs-recruitment.md).

### General Humphey and the road to Orta

General Theo Humphey sits in the large room at the heart of the H13 compound,
across the hall from the shooting range. He is classed as a civilian, so he
won't attack until provoked — but do **not** shoot him before talking to him
(that turns him hostile and the information is lost), and expect him to turn on
you anyway if the conversation runs long. He is well armed and highly skilled,
so don't get caught unprepared.

A merc with sufficient Leadership can trick him into revealing details about
the research facility in **Orta (sector K4)** — a place developing "weapons
like you've never seen" (the Rocket Rifles). The reveal is logged in your
laptop journal, and Orta appears on your map. Orta itself, and what you can
squeeze out of the scientists there, is covered in the
[late game chapter](late-game.md).

### Alma's side quests

- **Kill the Bloodcats** — Auntie, in the I14 residential area, begs you to
  destroy the bloodcat lair **east of her house**; the cats have killed four
  children and the army won't act. Bring a full squad, automatic weapons or
  shotguns, and hollow-point ammo, and move in a tight group — a lone merc
  caught by bloodcats is in serious trouble. Reward: Alma loyalty.
- **Free Dynamo from Tixa** — head miner Matt Duncan asks you to break his
  brother Greg "Dynamo" Duncan out of the hidden Tixa prison. Completing it
  boosts Alma's loyalty and gets you a cheap recruit ($50/day, or free if you
  refuse his first offer). Tixa is late-game territory — see
  [Estoni, Orta, Tixa and beyond](late-game.md).
- Random **bounty targets** from Intercept's Most Wanted can spawn in H14 and
  I14 — see [Side quests](side-quests.md) for the full terrorist-hunt quest.

## Sci-Fi mode: the bugs are stirring

If you started the campaign with Sci-Fi mode on, the mid game is usually when
the **Crepitus** wake up. The giant insects — created by the Queen's
experiments, much like the bloodcats — infest one of *your* mines once either
trigger fires: you capture **three or more mines**, or you stumble into the
hatch below Tixa prison that leads to a cavern full of larvae. The infested
mine is generally the first one you took, often Drassen.

You get some warning in the form of a cutscene where the Queen orders Elliot to
stop feeding bodies to the bugs. Then the head miner reports the infestation:
the mine stops producing until you clear the lair beneath it, all the way down
to the Crepitus Queen — every remaining bug dies the moment she does. Until you
do, the bugs raid at night, and losing a town sector to them costs civilian
lives and loyalty; leaving militia to auto-resolve against Crepitus is a
massacre, so keep a merc or two in the affected town.

Two sources of intel live in towns you now hold: **Ricardo "Rat" Grimaldo** in
Cambria G9 and his friend Gabby know what these creatures are. The lair assault
itself is covered in the [late game](late-game.md).

## Grumm

Five sectors: **G1, G2, H1, H2, H3**. Grumm is the heart of Arulcan industry —
a coastal factory town whose plants the regime converted into munitions mills.
Its mine (H3) is well defended but crucial for your progress. This is the
hardest of the three towns: barricaded houses, a machine-gunner in the factory
and a LAW-carrying soldier in the commercial district.

| Sector | What's there |
|--------|--------------|
| G1 | Residential district — chance to find Slay here |
| G2 | Warehouse district (fenced) |
| H1 | Commercial district — Fredo, Arnold, Tina |
| H2 | Munitions factory — Charlie's bar |
| H3 | Grumm mine and Mining Commission building |

### Taking the town

- **G1 (residential)**: houses wall-to-wall with little foliage. Many soldiers
  sit *inside* the houses and will interrupt your mercs walking past windows;
  another group barricades a house at the central junction. One easily-missed
  soldier hides in a small fenced area among crates in the southeast. Pure
  urban warfare — clear it building by building.
- **G2 (warehouse district)**: a chainlink fence rings the whole sector with no
  openings, so bring wire cutters or a grenade. Most defenders are inside the
  two main buildings. Few lights and wide flat roofs make this an ideal night
  attack.
- **H1 (commercial)**: essentially one long street running northwest to
  southeast — a sniper's dream, if you position accordingly. One enemy near the
  center of town carries a **LAW** and will use it on the first merc he sees;
  he loiters near an open crate.
- **H2 (munitions factory)**: soldier groups sit both north and south of the
  factory, so watch your flanks. The factory itself is one huge room full of
  crates and assembly lines; the defenders inside will not come out no matter
  how loud the fight gets, and at least one carries a big machine gun — walking
  in through the small south door is a good way to get shot. Pop the vertical
  sliding doors open or blow a wall instead; the civilian workers generally
  stay away from the outer walls, but check anyway.
- **H3 (mine)**: the Mining Commission building is the only structure. The
  thick trees and boulders to the north give great cover but put you right on
  top of the garrison; the south and east approaches are more exposed but let
  you start the fight at a distance instead of with the whole sector on your
  head.

### People and services in Grumm

- **Alexander Fredo** (H1) repairs **electronics** — the man Perko sends you
  to.
- **Arnold Brunzwell** (H1) is a gunsmith and mechanic.
- **Tina Elzan** (H1) runs a fabrics shop.
- **Sammy "Charlie" Elgin** tends the bar in H2 — and he is the one target in
  the Intercept's Most Wanted terrorist hunt who is *always* present, always in
  this spot. Manny de Santos, the barboy, is one of the Santos brothers. See
  [Side quests](side-quests.md).
- **Richard "Slay" Ruttwen** — there is a chance of finding Slay, another
  wanted terrorist, sitting in a wheelchair in one of the G1 houses (he can
  also turn up in G2, in Cambria F9 or G8, or in Alma I14). He can be talked
  into a week of free service instead of decapitated for the bounty; the
  trade-offs — including the 1.13 INI option that lets him stay forever — are
  covered on the [Recruitable NPCs page](npcs-recruitment.md).

## Travel, SAM sites and the helicopter

Arulco's airspace is guarded by four **SAM sites**: **D2, D15, I8 and N4**. Any
sector inside a SAM's coverage is a no-fly zone for Skyrider's helicopter —
enter it and the chopper gets shot up, forcing repairs before it can fly again.
On the map screen, click **Show Airspace** (or press ++a++) to see coverage:
red sectors are hostile airspace, green are clear.

The mid-game towns sit deep in SAM coverage. The **I8** site is directly south
of Cambria's mine sector, making it a natural target while you are in the area.
The two northern sites, D2 and D15, may already be yours from the
[early game](early-game.md); N4 is the Meduna site and a late-game problem —
it is surrounded by layers of barbed wire and sandbags and backed by tanks.

Worth knowing about SAM sites:

- Once you have captured a SAM site, Skyrider tells you where the rest are and
  marks them on the map. (If you took your first site before ever finding
  Skyrider, he only shares the locations after you capture another one.)
- They are well guarded, but the guards often drop better-than-average gear.
- Each SAM sector hides a few trapped chests in a locked room — disarm the
  traps first, or just shoot the locks.
- Destroying the SAM control computer in tactical mode disables the site
  without capturing it — temporarily, since the enemy repairs it if they retake
  the sector.
- The army aggressively tries to retake SAM sites, and keeps trying. Train
  militia there — in 1.13 you can train militia up to veterans in city *and*
  SAM sectors, and roaming militia patrols can intercept attackers before they
  arrive (see [Militia](../playing/features/militia.md)).

!!! tip "Watch the roads for an ice cream truck"
    **Hamous**, an old Jagged Alliance hand, wanders Arulco's roads in a stolen
    ice cream truck — usually somewhere between Drassen and Cambria or between
    San Mona and Cambria. A merc with decent Leadership (A.I.M. mercs may have
    better luck than IMPs) can hire him for **$250/day, truck included**. His
    Marksmanship is respectable for the price, he is stealthy, and the truck
    doubles as ground transport. In 1.13 he carries a Hunter's Vest and has the
    Primitive and Heat Tolerant traits. Don't expect brains: his Wisdom is 35.

## Holding what you take

The mid game is a war on two fronts: taking towns and keeping them. Cambria's
central crossroads make it the Queen's favorite counterattack target; Alma's
sectors and Grumm's mine get hit too. Before you push on:

- Train town militia everywhere, and garrison or militia-cover the SAM sites
  you captured — see [Militia](../playing/features/militia.md).
- Keep your income safe: each mine's output scales with town loyalty, so finish
  the loyalty quests (Joey, the Hicks, Krott, the bloodcats).
- Re-equip from what mid-game enemies drop. Thanks to 1.13's item progression
  the opposition is now bringing serious weapons and armor — so should you.

When Cambria, Alma and Grumm fly your flag and Orta is marked on your map, you
are ready for the
[late game: Estoni, Orta, Tixa, Balime and Meduna](late-game.md).
